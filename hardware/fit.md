# Freddy mask eye fit — preliminary review (2026-09-27)

The mask is stationary in a skeleton's lap on the covered porch. These observations are for a removable behind-the-opening screen mount; no mount geometry is approved for printing yet.

## STEP-based eye geometry

I imported the mask STEP with the Shapr3D CAD connection and measured front-to-back clear sightlines on a 0.5 mm X/Z grid using [this repeatable analysis](analyze_mask_step.py). Coordinates below are in the STEP's millimetres; front is approximately negative Y. These are **projected clear apertures**, not caliper measurements of the printed rim or a guaranteed mount clearance.

| STEP head half | Clear X bounds | Clear Z bounds | Widest horizontal span | Tallest vertical span | Clear-area center | Largest fully clear circle |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Negative X | −66 to −3 mm | 168.5 to 223.5 mm | ~62.5 mm | ~54.5 mm | (−33, 194.9) mm | ~52.2 mm diameter at (−30, 196) mm |
| Positive X | 29 to 92 mm | 168.5 to 223.5 mm | ~62.5 mm | ~54.5 mm | (58.9, 194.9) mm | ~52.2 mm diameter at (56, 196) mm |

The clear-area centers are about 92 mm apart; the centers that maximize an unoccluded circular view are about 86 mm apart. Those are different aesthetic placement choices, not prescribed mounting-hole positions. The mask aperture is wider than it is high. The nominal 2.1-inch display circle is about 53 mm, so a perfectly full, unobstructed display circle is unlikely at every angle; a narrow, deliberate edge overlap may look more integrated. In a coarse 2 mm scan of an untilted 75 mm disc centered at either largest-circle point, the head shell extends rearward to approximately Y = −20.5 mm near the upper rim. The shell depth varies strongly around the opening, making the adjustable rear cradle and tapered cosmetic surround useful.

For a **candidate straight-on CAD pose**, I rotated the official board model so its display faces toward negative Y, placed its center at (X, Z) = (−30, 196) or (56, 196) mm, and checked the 75 mm circular body plus the main display/PCB/standoff solids against the corresponding STEP head half. At board-origin Y = −18 mm the negative-X 75 mm body still intersects the shell by about 7 mm³; at Y = −17 mm that body no longer intersects. At Y = −15 mm, none of the selected major board solids intersects either head half. This is a starting position only: it does not quantify minimum surface gap, include all 513 vendor sub-solids or plugged-in cables, prove the 3D model matches the Rev2.0 units, or prove a good off-axis view. The chosen powered appearance may require changing center, tilt, or setback and rechecking interference.

## Provisional mount approach

Use two independent, removable rear cradles or retainers, one per eye, with the lit display centered behind the visible opening. Support the module by its housing/PCB mounting points with nonconductive spacers; do not clamp the glass, touch surface, display ribbon, or components. Keep a small adjustable setback so the display does not bear on the uneven printed rim. Provide access to the eye boards' `USB` ports for service/power and to the 4-pin UART headers for camera RX/common ground; leave the separate UART USB-C ports unplugged because they disable the headers. Route wires with strain relief and clearance from the brow/bridge/muzzle. The final print should be split or removable so either eye can be replaced without removing the whole mask.

Joshua's chosen appearance is **integrated with the mask**, rather than an exposed deep black socket. Pair each rear cradle with a separate, removable cosmetic eye surround that follows the irregular printed opening and visually carries the mask surface inward toward the lit circle. An opaque, matte inner wall should conceal the open cavity, gray display bezel, wiring, and any visible indicator light; finish its visible face to match the mask's eventual paint/texture. Leave a narrow, even dark reveal at the display edge instead of pressing trim against the glass or covering active pixels. The surround may need a tapered/stepped profile because the printed rim and display are at different depths. Keep the two eyes independently serviceable, and allow small center/tilt/setback adjustments before locking their positions.

This design direction now has [dimensioned first-fit carrier CAD](eye_mount/README.md). It is still a prototype, not print-fit approval: its separate trim annulus is only an appearance gauge, not the final tapered surround. Avoid bonding the PCB directly to the glued center seam. Do not glue or cut the printed mask based on the 2.48 in front annotation alone. A first print should be a low-cost test fit of one eye, not the finished pair.

## Remaining checks before mount CAD

1. Confirm the STEP export is the same scale and assembly state as the printed mask. The CAD removes the need to estimate the two projected eye silhouettes by hand, but glue, print tolerance, and assembly alignment can change the real fit.
2. Register the official board 3D assembly against each STEP head half at a chosen center, tilt, rotation, and setback; test interference with the shell, brow, bridge, and muzzle. The 75 mm reference-disc check above is only a conservative starting envelope.
3. Verify the **actual** board's rear mounting-point/thread geometry and its powered visible circle against the vendor CAD, then reserve space for the inserted USB and UART plugs, cable bend, and strain relief. Those cables are absent from both CAD models.
4. With both boards temporarily held at the selected positions, compare straight-on powered views at normal visitor distance with close-ups. Confirm the finish/color of the mask and whether the thin intentional display-edge overlap and cosmetic surround achieve the desired integrated appearance.

The first-fit rear carrier/pad/sleeve set is now modeled and clears both mask head halves in CAD. The official vendor assembly also clears the carrier and trim at this candidate pose; the printed Rev2.0 boards and plugged cables still need checking. Print one test set, verify both physical sides and the powered front appearance, then finalize the retained cosmetic surrounds and service removal. One eye has photographed powered placement and plugs inserted; the final center/rotation/setback, second eye, repeatable attachment, fixed cable routing, and strain relief remain unverified. Do not size the final surround from these perspective photos alone.
